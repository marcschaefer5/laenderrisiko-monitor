The basics · from one news event to one warning

One line of news. Sixty-one fields.

GDELT reads the world's news every fifteen minutes and writes down what it thinks happened, as a row of numbers and codes. This page takes one real row — recorded on 1 February 2025 and used in this thesis — and pulls it apart, field by field, until what is left is the number a model can learn from.

01   The record

Identity
1223954814 · 20250201
A global event id and the day it happened. Every row in GDELT carries both, which is what makes the archive addressable at all.
Who acted, on whom
REB → COD
Actor codes. REB is a rebel group, COD the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Ten further actor fields — ethnicity, religion, known group — stay empty in most rows.
What happened · CAMEO
192 · root 19 · quad 4
The event is classified into one of roughly 300 CAMEO codes. 192 is „occupy territory“, its root 19 is the family „fight“, and quad class 4 means material conflict. This is the field the risk thesaurus of the thesis filters on.
How severe · Goldstein
−9.5
A fixed value per CAMEO code, −10 to +10. Not measured from this event — it is the textbook severity of that kind of event.
How loud · attention
63 mentions · 9 sources
How often it was mentioned, by how many distinct outlets. Aggregated, this becomes the strongest predictor in both models — not violence, but the density of reporting.
How it reads · tone
−5.68
Average tone of the covering articles, roughly −100 to +100. The feature itself is negative, so a negative coefficient means: more negative tone raises the warning.
Where · geocoding
GM · 51.5, 10.5
Geocoded to Germany at the country centroid — although the article is about fighting in Goma. Rows like this are why a strict geographic filter and a domain blacklist run first.
Where it came from
npr.org …
One source url per row. It is what makes a row checkable by hand — and what the domain blacklist works on.

02   Six million become 730

What the model actually sees
6,237,331 events after filtering

Individual events are never shown to the model. They are counted, averaged and summed per country and day. 365 days × 2 countries leaves 730 rows — and that table is the entire model input.

Seven per country-day. Everything the two models ever see about a country is in these seven numbers.

04   Unusual, against its own normal

Germany and the USA are not comparable in absolute numbers — there are 27 American events for every German one. So each value is expressed as a distance from that country's own rolling 60-day normal. Crossing 1.5 standard deviations within the next three days is what the model is trained to anticipate.

05   Where the line is drawn

The model returns a probability. A human decides when it counts as a warning.

Germany warns at 0.20
USA warns at 0.60
0.00 · calmmodelled probability1.00 · certain
Germany · warning days / episodes / recall150 · 24 · 1.00
USA · warning days / episodes / recall43 · 10 · 0.75

Both thresholds come from the same rule — highest F1 on out-of-fold predictions. The rule produced two different answers, and that is the central result of the thesis in one picture: where the line sits is a decision about the cost of a false alarm.

And then the honest part

Everything above describes how a warning is produced. It says nothing about whether the warning is any good. That question needs a benchmark and a target that does not come from the same data — which is what the rebuild after submission supplied, and where the answer changed.
