Why eight countries and not twenty-one. The event features exist for twenty-one countries. The target does not: a daily risk index needs a freely traded currency, because a rate that is pegged or officially set does not move when the risk does. Six countries have a genuine market rate — Israel, Russia, Ukraine, Nigeria, Pakistan, Taiwan — and Germany and the USA run alongside them on the volatility of their own equity index. Eleven were excluded by exchange-rate regime: Mali and Iraq are pegged to the euro or the dollar; Lebanon, Syria, Yemen, Sudan, Somalia, Iran, Venezuela and Myanmar have officially set rates that do not track the real one; Ethiopia's series is half administered after the 2024 regime change; Afghanistan has standstill quotes on 23 % of days. The automated check passed Mali as usable — 9 % standstill days, a euro correlation of 0.14 — but the CFA franc is pegged to the euro, and a series that does not follow its own anchor is not a market rate. It was removed by hand.

The placebo tests

Each test asks one question. Green: the finding holds. Grey: it correctly stays silent. Red: it breaks. On a phone the chart scrolls sideways.

One finding survives both placebos, and no more than that. On the 14 documented events the warning does rise measurably. It does not survive a shift of the threshold or a split of the period — so it is evidence that the signal exists, not that it can be relied on operationally. Keeping those two apart is the whole point of the rebuild.

D26_anchor_baserate_test.py · daten/ankerereignisse.json · 20 anchors, each verified against a documented source

Did the warning come
before the event?

Fourteen documented events, seven per country, each with a dated source. A hit means the model was already above its warning threshold on the days before the event — not on the day itself.

Warning rate on the documented event days against every other day in the test window.

Same pipeline, two different kinds of usefulness. In the USA the event days stand clearly apart from ordinary days. In Germany they do not — because that model warns on seven days out of ten anyway, so a warning there carries almost no information.

D26_anchor_baserate_test.py · daten/ankerereignisse.json

GDELT's second
database

Correlation with the Economic Policy Uncertainty index, best feature of each database.

The measurement did get better — the answer did not change. GDELT publishes a second stream, the Global Knowledge Graph, which tags what is being talked about instead of what is being done. Against the established uncertainty index it correlates more than twice as strongly as the event counts the thesis is built on. So the suspected mismatch between construct and data was real. It was still not the reason for the flat result: the one market finding the themes produced did not survive its placebos, and the sample is too short to decide the question out of sample.

Counted news events do not anticipate uncertainty that is measured somewhere else. What they do describe is the present: how much is being reported, how negatively, and how that compares to a country's own recent normal. That is what the live monitor was built to show, and why every figure there stands next to its benchmark.
